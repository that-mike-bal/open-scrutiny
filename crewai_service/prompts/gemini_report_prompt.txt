QUALITATIVE RISK RUBRIC (REFERENCE FOR LLM)
===========================================

Classify risk **per category** using the definitions below. Do **not** produce any overall string-level score or probability. Anchor reasoning in ICANN string evaluation outcomes (similarity, singular/plural, name collision), policy/legal indicators, and cultural/linguistic factors.

A) Technical Compliance (DNS stability, protocol conformance, RZ‑LGR)
• Minimal: Passes stability/conformance; no material concerns.
• Low: Minor clarifications; standard remediation available.
• Moderate: Non‑critical gaps may delay evaluation; resolvable with changes.
• Concerning: Material issues likely to impede evaluation or require non‑standard mitigations.
• High: Expected adverse impact on DNS security/stability; cannot proceed without fundamental change.

B) Name Collision
• Minimal: No meaningful indicators; not designated high‑risk; no mitigation required.
• Low: Limited indicators; routine mitigations suffice.
• Moderate: Measurable traffic; defined mitigation plan and verification required; residual risk remains.
• Concerning: High‑risk designation or collision list but addressable with robust, time‑bound mitigation and commitments.
• High: High‑risk with ineffective/no mitigation; cannot proceed / termination likely.

C) String Similarity & Singular/Plural
• Minimal: Not Similar to any existing/applied‑for gTLDs/ccTLDs/blocked or two‑char ASCII; no singular/plural conflicts.
• Low: Similar only to another applied‑for string (same round) → contention possible but path forward exists.
• Moderate: Similar to a prior‑round applied‑for string → hold pending outcome.
• Concerning: Similar to existing ccTLDs/IDN ccTLDs/blocked names/two‑char ASCII or confirmed singular/plural with delegated/in‑process string requiring strong mitigations.
• High: Similar to existing delegated gTLD (outside applicant’s variant set) or singular/plural conflict that **cannot proceed** under program rules.

D) Safeguard Policy (GAC/PICs)
• Minimal: Only baseline PICs; no special safeguards implicated.
• Low: Group 1 considerations only; straightforward commitments.
• Moderate: Signals that may trigger heightened safeguards; manageable through documented PICs and monitoring.
• Concerning: Group 2/3 concerns likely; substantial commitments/oversight required.
• High: Strong GAC/IGO policy barriers likely to prevent delegation.

E) Geopolitical (Geographic Names)
• Minimal: Not a geographic name; no jurisdictional implications.
• Low: City‑level reference with available consent; low objection likelihood.
• Moderate: Regional references or multiple jurisdictions; consents needed; early warnings possible.
• Concerning: Country/territory implications without clear support; elevated objection risk.
• High: Clear country/territory requirements unmet; delegation unlikely.

F) Legal/Trademark
• Minimal: No relevant conflicts.
• Low: Weak/descriptive marks; low likelihood of successful LRO.
• Moderate: Mix of medium/strong marks with partial overlap; manageable via commitments.
• Concerning: Strong, well‑established marks in overlapping classes; elevated LRO risk.
• High: High probability of sustained objections (e.g., compelling LRO) likely to prevent delegation.

G) Cultural & Linguistic
• Minimal: Neutral meaning across major languages; no sensitivities.
• Low: Minor ambiguities/slang in limited locales; easily clarified.
• Moderate: Noticeable negative connotations in multiple regions/scripts; mitigations reduce but don’t remove risk.
• Concerning: Significant potential for offense/abuse in key markets; robust policies/outreach required.
• High: Pervasive adverse meaning or governmental sensitivity likely to trigger objections or block delegation.

Guidance for Flagging & Weighting
• When flags are raised, **name the specific counterpart(s)** (e.g., conflicting string ".sale"; mark **ACME SALES®**, USPTO Reg. ####, Nice Class 35) and cite the concrete trigger (similarity score, magnitude, group, jurisdiction).
• If a category is **High** due to program rules (e.g., Similarity cannot proceed), treat the application as BLOCKED in that category.
• A single isolated Concerning or High category among otherwise Minimal/Low categories does **not** automatically make the overall string HIGH or CRITICAL. Evaluate the **aggregate picture**.
• Missing data or "Data not available" should be flagged in Identified Gaps but must **not** inflate the risk classification. Absence of data ≠ presence of risk.

• Be specific and actionable in recommendations and assessments.
